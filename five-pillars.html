5 Pillars of Islam

The Five Pillars Explained Simply

The Five Pillars are the fundamental practices that every Muslim must follow. They are the framework of a Muslim's life.

These pillars support the Muslim community and help each person grow in trust, discipline, and compassion.

1. Shahada (Faith)

The declaration of faith: "La ilaha illallah, Muhammadur rasulullah" (There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah). Saying this sincerely is the first step in becoming a Muslim.

2. Salah (Prayer)

Praying five times a day facing the Kaaba in Mecca. It includes specific movements and recitations.

Prayer brings the believer back to Allah and creates a disciplined routine throughout the day.

3. Zakat (Almsgiving)

Giving a portion of wealth (usually 2.5%) to the poor and needy annually.

Zakat helps communities stay strong by sharing food, shelter, and support with those who need it most.

4. Sawm (Fasting during Ramadan)

Fasting from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan, abstaining from food, drink, smoking, and other physical needs.

Fasting teaches self-control, gratitude, and compassion for people who are hungry.

5. Hajj (Pilgrimage)

The pilgrimage to Mecca that every Muslim who is able must undertake at least once in their lifetime.

Hajj unites Muslims from every nation and reminds them of their shared faith and equal standing before Allah.

Why the Pillars Matter

These five practices are more than rules: they shape a Muslim's relationship with God, with others, and with the world around them.
